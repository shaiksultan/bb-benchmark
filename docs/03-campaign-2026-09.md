Source: the raw CSV folders `e_Fiber`, `e_5G_FWA`, `e_Mobile_5G`, and `Starlink`. Figures below are computed from those files. No 1.5×IQR filter was applied. The lab address stored in the `target` column is omitted here.

A later fiber-only folder, `new_e_Fiber`, covers 11–13 September and is not included in the tables.

Timestamps in the files are +04. Everything below is converted to UTC.

## Throughput

Mbps. iperf3 is the controlled test to the central server. Ookla is the public speed test. Medians are of passing numeric rows.

| Access | iperf3 down, median (n) | iperf3 up, median (n) | Ookla down, median (n) | Ookla up, median |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fiber | 713 (82) | 188 (82) | 899 (42) | 322 |
| 5G fixed wireless | see below (60 pass) | see below (67 pass) | 940 (37) | 57 |
| Mobile 5G | 328 (9) | 73 (8) | 340 (19) | 70 |
| LEO | 240 (26) | 12 (26) | 351 (26) | 35 |

Fiber iperf3 download ranged from 553 to 778 Mbps. One download and one upload were marked fail and are excluded.

### 5G fixed wireless needs two numbers, not one

Of 60 passing iperf3 downloads:

- 35 were under 1 Mbps
- 24 were at or above 100 Mbps, and those 24 have a median of 717 Mbps (maximum 744)

Of 94 download rows in total, 60 passed and the rest failed. The median of all passing downloads is 0.06 Mbps, because most of them transferred almost nothing. Quoting that median as the capacity of the access network would be misleading. Quoting only the 717 Mbps median would hide that most tests did not transfer.

Ookla on the same probe stayed near 940 Mbps (mean 940, minimum 926, maximum 949) across 37 tests. Public speed tests succeeded while a large share of tests to the central server did not. Until that path is explained, fixed-wireless capacity should be quoted from Ookla only with that label, and the central-server tests should be quoted as reachability to the lab, not as the access speed.

The draft figure of 1,310 Mbps iperf3 for fixed wireless is **not in these files**. The highest iperf3 download here is 744 Mbps.

### Mobile 5G sample is thin

Nine download tests in about 18 hours, one of them at 93 Mbps and the other eight between 307 and 332 Mbps. The median is 328 Mbps. That is the central-server result. It is not a 1.5 Gbps result.

The draft figures of 1,542 Mbps Ookla and 1,590 Mbps iperf3 are **not in these files**. The highest Ookla download in the mobile folder is 368 Mbps. If a Samsung Galaxy S25 handset test produced a gigabit-class result, it is a different method and has to be labeled as a handset test.

## Delay, loss, DNS

Medians of passing samples.

| Access | RTT average | UDP jitter | UDP loss | DNS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fiber | 11.1 ms | 0.23 ms | 0% | 8.2 ms |
| 5G fixed wireless | 21.2 ms | 3.4 ms | 99% | 22 ms |
| Mobile 5G | 30.5 ms | 1.8 ms | 0% | 26 ms |
| LEO | 48.1 ms | 1.8 ms | 0.04% | 50 ms |

DNS means are much higher than these medians (fiber mean is 49 ms against a median of 8 ms) because a minority of lookups are slow. Use the median.

Fixed-wireless UDP loss is two clusters, not a mild loss rate: 47 samples under 1%, and 72 samples at or above 90%. The 10 Mbps UDP stream often did not arrive. Combined with 57.9% probe success, this describes the path to the lab during this campaign, not a clean access-loss KPI.

LEO UDP loss median is 0.04%. The mean is 0.6% because of a long tail (maximum 38%).

## Voice estimate and SIP

| Access | Estimated MOS, median | Synthetic SIP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fiber | 4.41 (160 samples) | 0 of 3,186 calls completed, port 5062 |
| 5G fixed wireless | 3.43 (131 samples) | 0 of 3,626 calls completed, port 5063 |
| Mobile 5G | 4.40 (68 samples) | 0 of 1,403 calls completed, port 5064 |
| LEO | 4.39 (184 samples) | 3,178 of 3,646 calls completed (87%), port 5061 |

The MOS column is the formula in the methodology note. Fiber, mobile, and LEO sit on the formula's ceiling. The fixed-wireless median is lower because the loss term fires on the failed UDP tests.

SIP completed only on the LEO port. The other three ports returned failure for every run, including the short 3-call checks and the 20-call runs. That pattern fits a listener that was not accepting the scenario on those ports. It is not evidence that fiber, fixed wireless, or mobile voice service failed for customers.

## Browser timing

Headless Chromium ran about every 15 minutes on fiber, mobile 5G, and LEO. The fixed-wireless folder has no browser file.

On fiber (482 samples) and mobile 5G (211 samples), every operator-site load was marked pass with HTTP 200, and every page title was `Request Rejected`. The timer is measuring a block page, not the rendered homepage. Do not publish those durations as homepage load time. The news-site load failed on a large share of attempts (fiber 155 of 480, mobile 100 of 211).
